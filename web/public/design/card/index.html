Design exploration, sample data

The member card

Three directions for the N10 card on the web, and how each would carry into Apple and Google Wallet. The name, points and codes are invented. The Wallet pictures are mock-ups of what we would send, not real passes.

Today’s card, honestly

  • The code shouts, the points whisper. The four-digit code is the biggest thing on the card; a guest cares first about their points, and staff scan the QR instead of reading the code.
  • The venue is named twice. The N10 crest already carries the wordmark, and the full name “N10 Shisha Lounge by Hydur” is set again in gold, shrunk and wrapped to fit beside it.
  • Progress is nearly invisible. A 0.7% tall bar on the bottom edge; the reward-ready state only changes one small label.
  • A light follows the pointer. That is the kind of effect our design rules rule out, and on a phone it does nothing.
  • Small labels in tracked capitals everywhere (Member, Your code, Points). They add texture, not information.
  • Some text is too small. At the width of a phone (about 360 px) the smallest labels are about 7 px. The directions below keep every line at 11 px or more on a phone.
  • The QR is a little small at about 22% of the card width. It still read well in our scan test, but staff phones work from further away.

A. Ledger

Mid-way state: 240 of 500.

The idea: it reads like a private account. The guest’s name leads, the points sit beneath it like a balance, and one gold line shows how far they are. The crest replaces the written venue name.

For staff: the QR grows to 26% of the width, with the code under it for typing when a camera fails.

Risk: quiet. It relies on good marble and good type; it does not perform.

B. Counter

Mid-way state: 240 of 500, ten marks of 50 points.

The idea: built for the moment at the till. A bone stub holds the code and the QR, the marble side holds the guest’s name and progress, in ten marks.

For staff: the code is readable across the counter and the QR sits on a large light field, the easiest of the three to scan.

Risk: a third of the card is bright. It starts to look like a ticket rather than a black card.

C. Seal

The idea: the front is only crest, name and one line of progress, centred like a seal. The code lives on the back: the guest taps the card to turn it over at the till.

For staff: the back is the largest QR of the three, on a full bone field.

Risk: one extra tap at a busy counter, and guests have to learn it. The line in the Wallet strip would need a new picture for every points change.

Recommendation: A, Ledger

Why: it fixes the hierarchy (name, then points, then code), keeps the QR on the front where staff expect it, and stays a black card. It carries into Wallet without tricks: the strip is plain marble, the fields do the talking.

Borrowed from the others: C’s back as a full-screen “Show my code” view on the card page, for staff whose phones struggle; B’s larger code figures if staff type codes often.

Needs from Saad: whether the crest may stand in for the written name on the card (it already contains “N10 by Hydur”), and the real reward wording.
